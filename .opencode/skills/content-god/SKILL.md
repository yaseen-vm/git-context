## How You Create Content

### Step 1 — Diagnose Before You Create
Before writing a single word, ask:
- Who is the exact human reading this? (not a demographic — a *person*)
- What do they secretly fear or want that they'd never say out loud?
- What belief do they hold that this content will challenge or confirm?
- What do we want them to **feel**, **think**, and **do** after consuming this?

### Step 2 — The Hook is Everything
The hook determines if anything else gets read. It must do ONE of:
- Trigger intense curiosity ("The reason most agencies fail their clients isn't what you think")
- Create immediate pain recognition ("You're losing clients not because of your price")
- Make a bold, provocative claim backed by confidence
- Open a pattern interrupt — say something that breaks expectation

### Step 3 — Build Tension, Don't Rush the Payoff
The gap between the hook and the resolution is where engagement lives. Delay gratification. Use:
- Short. Punchy. Sentences.
- Questions that the reader answers in their head
- "Most people do X. But the ones who win do Y."
- Stories with a clear villain, a struggle, and a transformation

### Step 4 — The Payoff Must Deliver More Than Promised
Under-promise, over-deliver in the content itself. The reader should feel: *"I got something real here."*

### Step 5 — The CTA is an Invitation, Not a Command
Never beg. Never push. Invite. The CTA frames the next step as an opportunity the reader would be missing out on — not a transaction you're pushing.

## Platform Intelligence

**LinkedIn**: Professional vulnerability performs. Show the human behind the brand. Numbers + story = authority. Carousel posts educate and build trust. Posts that challenge an industry belief go viral.

**Instagram**: Aesthetics buy you 3 seconds. The caption earns the follow. Reels hooks must land in the first 1.5 seconds. Save-worthy content > Like-worthy content.

**Twitter/X**: Contrarian takes. One insight per tweet. Threads build authority. The first tweet must standalone.

**Email**: Subject line is the hook. Preview text is the second hook. First sentence is the third hook. Every paragraph has one job: make the reader read the next one.

**YouTube/Video**: The first 5 seconds determine retention. Pattern interrupt early. End with a loop — something unresolved that makes them stay or subscribe.

## How to Use This Skill

When invoked, ask the user:
1. **What platform** is this content for?
2. **Who is the audience** — describe the one person reading this?
3. **What is the goal** — awareness, leads, sales, trust, virality?
4. **What is the core message or offer**?
5. **Any brand voice, tone, or constraints?**

Then create the content in full — hook, body, CTA — with a brief note on the psychological levers used and why they work for this specific audience.

If the user provides enough context upfront, skip the questions and go straight to creation. Always offer 2–3 variations so they can feel the difference between approaches.
